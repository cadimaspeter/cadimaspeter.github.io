01

Project Overview

This project focused on transforming an existing CRM into a more organized, usable, and actionable lead-management system for a real estate business. The objective was not simply to organize records — it was to improve the business's ability to understand where leads stood, what needed attention, and what action should happen next.

As the Customer Relationship Manager on this engagement, the scope covered CRM organization, data cleanup, pipeline structuring, and lead-tracking standardization within Brevo.

02

The Challenge

The existing CRM contained valuable lead information, but the data and pipeline structure could be refined to make lead management more consistent, visible, and actionable.

  • Inconsistent lead information across records
  • Duplicate or incomplete contact entries
  • Leads without clear or consistent statuses
  • Leads sitting in inappropriate pipeline stages
  • Inconsistent naming conventions
  • Missing contact information
  • Unclear ownership of individual leads
  • Difficulty distinguishing active leads from stale ones
  • Inconsistent follow-up processes
  • Lack of standardized CRM fields
  • Limited pipeline visibility for reporting
The existing CRM contained valuable lead information, but the data and pipeline structure could be refined to make lead management more consistent, visible, and actionable.
03

CRM Audit

Before any cleanup began, the CRM was fully audited across three layers: lead records, pipeline structure, and data quality.

Lead Records

Reviewed contact names, emails, phone numbers, company information, lead source, lead status, assigned owner, notes, activity history, missing information, and duplicate records.

Pipeline

Reviewed pipeline stages, leads within each stage, incorrectly categorized leads, stale opportunities, leads requiring follow-up, already-converted leads, and inactive leads.

Data Quality

Analyzed duplicate records, missing fields, inconsistent formatting, outdated information, incomplete records, incorrect statuses, and inconsistent tags or categories.

04

Lead Data Cleanup

CRM records were reviewed and organized with the goal of preserving valuable lead information while improving overall data quality.

  • Identifying duplicate contacts
  • Consolidating duplicate information into single records
  • Standardizing names and contact information
  • Correcting inconsistent entries
  • Updating missing information where available
  • Removing irrelevant records where appropriate
  • Identifying and separating inactive leads from active ones
  • Reviewing and standardizing lead sources and categories
  • Updating lead statuses to reflect current reality
Audit → Identify Issues → Clean → Standardize → Verify → Organize
05

Pipeline Structure

The lead pipeline was reviewed and organized into clear stages that represent the customer journey from first contact through conversion.

New Lead → Contacted → Qualified → Follow-Up
→ Appointment / Consultation → Proposal / Opportunity → Converted → Closed / Lost

Standardized pipeline stages were used to:

  • Make lead status immediately visible
  • Identify the next required action
  • Reduce forgotten or overlooked leads
  • Improve follow-up consistency
  • Make reporting easier
  • Help prioritize opportunities
  • Create a consistent, repeatable sales process
06

CRM Workflow

The cleaned CRM was structured to support a repeatable lead-management process, creating accountability and visibility throughout the lead lifecycle.

Lead Enters CRM → Lead Categorized → Lead Assigned
→ Lead Contacted → Status Updated → Follow-Up Scheduled
→ Lead Progresses → Converted / Nurtured / Closed
